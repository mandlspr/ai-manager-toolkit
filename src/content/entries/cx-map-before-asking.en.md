## Principle

Before asking colleagues how a system works, build your own map of what is already there. Inventory it, structure it, make the shape visible. Then bring people in.

## Why it matters

The reason is cost, not method. A stakeholder interview spends someone else's time and attention, and spending it on questions you could have answered by looking is a poor trade. A map also changes what you are able to hear. It gives you a factual baseline against which to compare what people say they do, and without one you have no way of noticing the gap between the described workflow and the one that exists.

## How to apply it

- Inventory the existing setup first, in whatever form makes its structure visible.
- Note what is duplicated, dormant or missing. Imbalance is easier to see on a map than in a list.
- Come to interviews with specific questions rather than open discovery.
- Treat any gap between the map and the account as a finding, not something to correct on the spot.

## Point of caution

A map tells you what exists, not what hurts. Only the people using the system can tell you that.

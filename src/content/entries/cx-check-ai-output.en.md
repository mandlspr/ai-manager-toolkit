## Principle

Four signals should stop you before an AI output goes anywhere. They fire at different moments and catch different failures, so none of them replaces the others.

## Why it matters

The fourth is the one most people lack. An output can be factually correct and still be empty: a confident claim that says nothing, a formulation that sounds like a system rather than a person. Fluency hides it and accuracy checks miss it. The first signal fires before you have read anything. If the question has no reliable sources or examples behind it, distrust is the right starting position rather than the conclusion.

## How to apply it

- Before reading: if no reliable source or example exists for the question asked, treat the answer as suspect.
- Against a known result: where a calculation has been done before, an implausible output is the cheapest thing to catch.
- Against what you had in mind: a mismatch counts as a signal even if you had not articulated the intent yet.
- Against tone: if it rings hollow, ask for a rewrite. Correct and empty is still a failure.

## Point of caution

A tool's statement about its own limits is a claim, not a fact. "I don't have access to that" can be a hallucination, particularly when you know by other means that the access exists. Check a declared limitation against what you know of the tool rather than against how plausible the sentence sounds.

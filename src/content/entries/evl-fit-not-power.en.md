## Principle

More powerful is not more accepted. Two findings point the same way. A flawless system feels threatening: when the AI performs better, people choose it less. And personalisation has a peak — resemblance helps, then becomes uncomfortable and effectiveness falls. Fit is the target. Power is not.

## Why it matters

Capability is what vendors sell and what evaluations measure. But at the point of use, what decides is whether the system matches the task and the person. So a stronger option can make the outcome worse.

## How to apply it

- Set the level the task needs, then stop. A stronger option is a hypothesis to test, never a default upgrade.
- *Own practice: AI models are routed by task — a balanced level by default, the highest only for long multi-file work. The strongest setting is not the safe setting.*
- Treat personalisation as a dial with a peak, not one to push to maximum.
- Automate the functional work first; people accept it there far better than on what feels personal to them.

## Point of caution

This evidence measures acceptance, not quality of work. It says a well-fitted system is better received — not that a weaker one does a better job. *Is this enough for the task?* is a governance question. *Will people accept it?* is a design question. You need both.

## Principle

Handling a large volume of material is not a discipline problem. Build the protocol and the automation that collect it: a naming convention decided before the first file, named sources with explicit trigger conditions, and a single agreed destination.

## Why it matters

Discipline runs out at volume. Anyone can name files consistently for a week, but over months and across several sources it breaks down, and what breaks first is the naming, which is exactly what makes the archive usable later. Moving the work upstream removes the question of whether you had the energy that day. It also means material arrives already structured, so the sorting pass at the end is a real pass rather than a rescue.

## How to apply it

- Decide the naming convention with whoever, or whatever, will be doing the filing.
- Name the sources to collect and their trigger conditions: scheduled, on an event, or on explicit request.
- Say when not to trigger. A task that runs on content not yet available produces noise you will pay for later.
- Send output to one agreed destination.
- Keep a constant metadata schema — where it came from, when, what it relates to. The file formats themselves stay mixed, and an indexing layer upstream absorbs that.
- Keep the sorting pass as its own task.
- Mark what is too complex or that you know won't stick, rather than skipping it. A flagged gap is recoverable; a silent one isn't.

## Point of caution

You can build the system that documents a handover. Whether the handover held afterwards isn't something you're in a position to verify.

## Context

A DTC fashion company, roughly fifteen people and a 3PL partner, selling to both consumers and trade. The support tool had been installed four years earlier by a colleague and never revisited. One agent account served two audiences, the macro library was almost entirely oriented to one of them, there was no naming convention and no performance tracking, and returns were handled on a separate mailbox outside the tool. A separate mapping project, tools against customer journey stages, had already made that last point visible: an entire customer channel was running outside the support system.

## What was done

Seven months, solo, in sequence. The existing macro library was inventoried and visualised before anything was touched. Every user of the tool was then interviewed in a structured 45-minute session covering current workflow, friction, ideal state, and one short closing question about what could change immediately. That last question surfaced the returns channel as a visibility problem rather than a process one, which reframed it as a decision about where data should live. The rebuild followed: twelve groups replacing an unstructured library, separate auto-replies and tone per audience, satisfaction measurement activated for the first time, the returns mailbox routed into the tool with keyword-based assignment in three languages, and product reviews pulled into a single satisfaction view. Four integrations were evaluated and not implemented, three blocked by plan cost and one judged unsuitable for the business model. Each was documented with its reason rather than dropped, and the budget constraint was read as a signal about priority rather than an obstacle to push against.

## Result

First response time fell from roughly thirteen hours to as low as two, against a published commitment of twenty-four. Satisfaction stood at 94% at the time of departure. The returns channel, previously invisible to the support system, became measurable within it. Everything was documented across three tools — process documentation, a returns catalogue, an onboarding guide, and the versioned maps — with a compensation policy co-written with the e-commerce lead. What happened to the system afterwards cannot be verified from here and is not claimed.

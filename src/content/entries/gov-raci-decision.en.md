## Principle

Every AI decision needs one accountable owner. RACI names four roles: Responsible executes, Accountable decides and signs off, Consulted is the expert asked, Informed is told — one Accountable per task, the use-case owner, or leadership for a risk classification. Governance runs a repeating six-step cycle — capture, classify, check, decide, enable, monitor — restarted by any change of intended use.

## Why it matters

"Who decides" is the gap a product-lifecycle view and a customer-signal loop both leave open. Without a single Accountable, a risk classification has no owner and shadow AI fills the space. In the German market the most-cited cause of failure for an internal chatbot — ahead of data protection — is the works council, a standing party under §87 BetrVG, brought in early rather than once the tool is built.

## How to apply it

- Map R, A, C, I across business, IT/security, DPO, legal, procurement and leadership at project start.
- Enforce one Accountable per task; two names means it is not yet owned.
- Put the works council on the list from the first step, and re-map on every change of use.

## Point of caution

RACI names who decides, not what is decided; it sequences the classification and the privacy and security checks, it does not replace them.

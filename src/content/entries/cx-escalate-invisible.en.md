## Principle

The trigger for escalation is not complexity. It is holding context the system cannot access by construction, and that changes the answer.

## Why it matters

A classification system works on fixed criteria applied to what is recorded. It cannot see the state of a relationship, an external factor the customer does not yet know about, the seniority of the person asking, or a pattern visible only across an account's history. When any of these makes the recorded case misleading, no threshold will catch it. This is what the human in the loop is for: not re-doing the system's work more slowly, but supplying what the system has no field for.

## How to apply it

- Escalate when the relationship history contradicts what the ticket says.
- Escalate when you hold an external factor the customer cannot know but that bears directly on their question.
- Note who is asking, not only what is asked. The same request from a different level of the organisation is a different request.
- Write down which signal triggered the escalation. That record is what lets the fixed criteria be improved later.

## Point of caution

This judgement is not auditable by default. Unless the signal is written down, an escalation based on context looks arbitrary from the outside, and cannot be used to refine the criteria that missed it.
